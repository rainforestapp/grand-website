A few discreet sensors that learn her ordinary day and tell you the moment something's off. No cameras. No wearables. Nothing to charge.

Everything is probably fine… right?

Your mom lives alone, and that's not going to change. She's doing great — more friends than you, book club, hobbies. But she did have a fall last year. And every morning there's a small thought at the back of your mind: what if something happened and I just don't know it yet?

The alternatives

You've probably looked at the options. Every one of them asks her to change. Grand doesn't.

A pendant or watch

Only works if she remembers to charge it and wears it all the time. Hers was on the nightstand the morning she fell.

A call-for-help button

If she does fall, it probably won't be within reach when she needs it.

An in-home carer

Expensive, and she doesn't want a stranger in her home.

A camera

She'd resent you for even considering it. And you don't want to watch her anyway.

A small hub. A few sensors. That's the whole thing.

Plug them in, and you're done. There's nothing for her to wear, remember, press, or charge — which is exactly why it works on the morning she forgets.

The hub

Processes everything in the home. It tells you she's fine, or pings you when something's off. No audio is ever sent to the cloud.

The sensors

Small enough to disappear. They plug into any outlet and use motion and audio detection to notice the shape of her day — and to hear a fall.

It learns what an ordinary day looks like — and flags the day that isn't.

Not a live feed. A quiet sense of whether today looks like every other day.

Indicators of a fall

Sudden changes in motion or audio that may mean an emergency. When that happens, a real person from Grand reaches out to confirm she's okay.

Open the app. See today at a glance.

Is the day broadly normal, or is there something worth a check-in? That's the only question the app answers — tuned to avoid false alarms, and never a live view into her home.
